//6.7 — Turnaround vs. response time: worked example showing no ordering wins both

Take two CPU-bound processes, both arriving at t=0, each with a burst of 10 units.

---

Approach A
— run one to completion, then the other (like FCFS/SJF, no preemption): P1 then P2.

response time:

p1 -> 0 as it start immediatly , trunaround time 10
p2 -> 10 wait for p1 to finish , turnaround time is 20

Average response = (0+10)/2 = 5.
Average turnaround = (10+20)/2 = 15.

---

Approach B — Round Robin, quantum = 1,
alternating P1, P2, P1, P2...

p1 -> response time 0 , turnaround time 19
p2 -> response time 1 , turnaround time 20

Average response = (0+1)/2 = 0.5.
Average turnaround = (19+20)/2 = 19.5.

NoticeL
batch approach methoda A has better avg turnaround time , but method B has better avg response

RR crushes response time (everyone gets a near-instant first turn) but inflates turnaround time for everyone, because constant interleaving means no process actually finishes any sooner in wall-clock terms — they're all stretched out together. Batch execution gets someone (P1) finished fast and keeps average turnaround low, but only by making the other process (P2) wait completely idle before it even starts.
