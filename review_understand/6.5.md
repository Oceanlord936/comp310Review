//6.5 — SRTN (preemptive SJF) and Round Robin — quantum trade-offs

SRTN (Shortest Remaining Time Next)

is SJF's preemptive cousin. Where plain SJF only picks at moment 1/4 (a process finishes or blocks, then you pick the next shortest),

SRTN also checks at moment 3 (Waiting→Ready, from 6.2): whenever a new process arrives in the ready queue, compare its burst length to the remaining time of whatever's currently running.

Notice:
If the new arrival is shorter than what's left of the current job, preempt immediately and switch to it.

---

but it costs more context switches / dispatch overhead (from 6.1), and it still needs burst-length prediction (6.4's exponential averaging), since "remaining time" for the running process is estimate-minus-elapsed.

==============

Round Robin (RR) — the one xv6 actually runs. Instead of ranking by burst length at all, RR gives every process a fixed time slice, the quantum (or time quantum), in FIFO order.

A process runs until either it finishes/blocks on its own (moment 1/4), or the quantum timer expires (moment 2 — preemptive). If it hasn't finished, it goes to the back of the ready queue and the next process in line gets a turn. No burst-length knowledge needed at all — that's RR's whole appeal: it's prediction-free and treats every process as equally deserving of a fair, repeating turn.

---

The quantum trade-off is the central design tension:

1. Quantum too large → RR degenerates toward FCFS (each process basically runs to completion before the timer ever fires), so you inherit FCFS's convoy-effect risk and poor response time for short/interactive jobs.

2. Quantum too small → you get excellent response time (everyone gets a turn almost instantly), but you pay dispatch latency (6.1) on every single switch. If the quantum approaches the size of a context switch, throughput collapses — the CPU spends more time switching than doing useful work.

============================================

Notice:

two different rules for two different trigger moments —

1. New arrival while something's running → compare newcomer only against current runner's remaining time.

2. Running process leaves (finishes/blocks) → scan the whole ready queue for the true minimum.
