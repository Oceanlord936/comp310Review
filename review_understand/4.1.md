//4.1: Why xv6 draws the address space differently from the lecture

The lecture picture is the textbook generic layout.
From the bottom up it goes:
text, initialized data, BSS, heap growing up, a free gap, then the stack at the top growing down.

xv6 is a small teaching kernel, so it simplifies that picture.
Here are the five differences.

======================

REAL XV6 / gini

1. the stack is below the heap . the heap sits above the stack and grows up

2. the stack is one fixed pages (4096 bytes) for the life of teh process

3. only the heap grows , upwrd and away from teh stack . nothing meets in teh middle

4. There is one data band . exec
   loads teh ELF data segment , and its in memeory size already inclided the zeored BSS

5. xv6 has a fixed array of 64 struct_porn slots
   so tehre is hard cap of 64 process in stack

---

Why the layout is this way???

exec builds the image once:
text, data, a guard page, then one stack page. Everything above that is heap.
Since the stack never grows, xv6 needs no stack-growth logic. It puts an unmapped-for-user guard page below the stack, so an overflow faults immediately instead of silently corrupting the data region. That's the subject of 4.3.

Row 5 (struct_porc)is a different kind of difference. It's about the kernel's process table, not the address space, but the plan groups it here because it's the same "xv6 picks the simple, fixed option" pattern. It ties back to 3.5.

The takeaway. The lecture picture explains the concepts. The xv6 picture is what the GINI faces actually show you, so when they disagree, xv6 is the one to trust for T01 and T02.
