// 3.6 Scheduling queues

The kernel keeps processes in queues according to what they're waiting for.

---

Queue types

1. job queue:
   every process in system

2. ready queue :
   process that are in memeory and ready to run , waiting for the cpu.
   the scheduler pickes thenext one from here

3. Event (device / wait) queues: processes blocked on a particular event. There is one queue per event or device (disk, keyboard, a child's exit, and so on).

// The four paths a process takes after running (the classic four; check them against your lecture slide):

1. Requests I/O: goes to that device's event queue, and returns to the ready queue when the I/O completes.
2. Time slice expires: it is preempted and goes straight back to the ready queue.
3. Creates a child and waits for it (fork then wait): goes to a wait queue until the child terminates.
4. Waits for an interrupt or other event: goes to an event queue until it happens.

==========================
Pattern to remember: a process never goes from waiting straight to running.
When its event happens, it moves to the ready queue and waits its turn.
