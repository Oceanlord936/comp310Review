//7.1 — spin and busy: CPU-bound load generators for the scheduler

# These are two of GINI's practice programs — not kernel code, just simple user programs

spin:
an infinite loop around a single instruction — effectively the PC (program counter) just freezes, jumping back to the exact same address over and over (e.g. while(1);). It never moves through memory. This is the simplest possible "hog the CPU" program: minimal, predictable, does no real work, useful for load-testing the scheduler itself (e.g. spawn three spin processes and watch how RR/priority/etc divides time between them) without any other variable in play.

==========

busy: also CPU-bound and never yields voluntarily, but it actually executes a real loop with a body — the PC visibly advances through a range of instructions each iteration rather than sitting on one address. It's still not doing anything meaningful computationally, but because the PC moves, it's more representative of what a "real" CPU-bound program looks like (as opposed to spin's degenerate single-instruction case), and it's a closer analogue to something like a compute-heavy loop.
