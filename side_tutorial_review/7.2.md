//7.2 — walker: makes the PC visibly march across memory pages

walker is designed to make program-counter movement across pages visible — rather than looping

---

To do this, walker needs more code/text than a normal small program would fit in one page — it needs its executable code laid out across multiple pages so there's actually something to walk across

That's what sbrkexec is for: it's a GINI-specific system call (not standard xv6/Unix) that grows a process's address space specifically to hold executable code in the newly allocated region

---

as opposed to the standard sbrk, which just grows the heap for data. walker uses sbrkexec to allocate extra pages, fill them with more code/instructions, and then have the PC actually jump into and execute across those pages

— demonstrating that code, not just data, can live in dynamically-grown memory, and letting you watch the page-table / PC-tracking machinery work in real time.

---

So the throughline: spin (PC fixed) → busy (PC moves within one page) → walker (PC moves across pages, requiring sbrkexec to have somewhere to walk to).
